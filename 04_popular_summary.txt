Popular Summary: Virtual-State Metrology in the Quantum Rabi Model

Superconducting circuits can couple artificial atoms and microwave light so strongly that their quietest state contains hidden, or virtual, excitations. We ask whether this dressing can improve precision measurements and compare it with a driven process that turns virtual excitations into real photons. Our calculations show a modest static enhancement and much larger idealized gains after driving. The decisive test is loss: real photons escape from the cavity, and the predicted gain falls below one percent at a representative coplanar-waveguide linewidth. A low-loss cavity preserves more of the signal, but the collective many-qubit calculation depends on the number of photon states retained and is not converged. These results distinguish information stored in virtual dressing from information carried by lossy real photons. They define a measurable coherence target for future experiments, rather than a device-performance claim.
